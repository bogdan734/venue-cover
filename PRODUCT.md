# Product

## Register

product

## Users

- **A renter** insuring one event at a venue they are renting. On a phone, in the evening, once. They want a price and a
  certificate, and to know that what they typed was understood.
- **An underwriter** working through referred events at a desk, many a day. They need the reasons, the working behind the
  price and the nearest past decisions on one screen, and a decision that takes a minute.
- **A partner's developer** wiring a venue's booking system to the API: events, webhooks, a reference they can trust.
- **A technical reviewer.** An engineering lead at an insurer or a managing general agent who opens the link between
  meetings and wants to follow one event through every area in a few minutes.

## Product Purpose

A showcase platform for event liability on venue rentals. A sentence goes in and a policy comes out, with a person wherever
judgement is needed. It exists to show the whole of that business working end to end: client setup, reading and pricing,
referral, issue, reports, partner APIs, and a measure of how often the automation is right. Success is a reviewer who can
follow one event through every area without help and finds the working shown at each step.

## Brand Personality

Plain, exact, calm. It speaks like a careful colleague: says what happened, shows the working, names what is simulated. No
sales voice, no cleverness. The feeling to leave is that nothing is hidden and nothing is decoration.

## Anti-references

- A SaaS dashboard of gradient hero numbers and identical icon cards.
- The cream-paper and serif template every generated site wears.
- A terminal-styled dark console: monospaced money, slashed zeroes, everything a log.
- An insurer's brochure: stock photos, shields, reassurance instead of information.

## Design Principles

1. **Show the working.** Every decision is followed by why: the text, the facts read, the match, the rule, the price split.
2. **A person is always in the picture.** Where the automation stops, the page says who has it and by when.
3. **Figures are for adding up.** One face for numbers, aligned, with totals that visibly sum.
4. **Two houses.** A portal belongs to the client program and looks like theirs: light, the program's name first. The back
   office is ours: a dark rail, dense, quiet.
5. **Say what is not real.** Invented data and simulated payment or mail are named where they appear.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.2 AA: text contrast 4.5:1 and above, every control reachable and operable by keyboard with a visible focus ring,
labels on every field, status never by colour alone. Motion is decoration only and stops under `prefers-reduced-motion`.
English first; descriptions are also read in French.
